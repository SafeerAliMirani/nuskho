Going to the medical store

دوائن جي دڪان تي وڃڻ

The brand list cannot come from a website. It comes from a shop that already knows the doctor. One good page of a pharmacy's own paperwork is worth more than a month of searching, because it is current, it is Larkana, and somebody there can say yes to it. This page is what to ask for and what to leave alone.

Go through the doctor, not around him

Start at the pharmacy that already fills his prescriptions: his hospital's own counter, or the shop below his clinic. A stranger asking a shopkeeper for his stock list is asking a competitor for his business. The doctor asking on your behalf is a favour between people who see each other daily, and it costs nobody anything. Ask the doctor first, then go.

What to ask for, best first

  1. 1The distributor's price list. A printed booklet or a file the distributor sends the shop, usually monthly. This is the single best item: hundreds of brands, correct strengths, already typed. The shop is giving away nothing of its own by sharing it, which is exactly why it is the easy ask.
  2. 2Last month's purchase invoices. What this shop actually bought, so what this town actually takes. Ask for a month. Two months is a good catalogue.
  3. 3Pages of the stock register. The book behind the counter. Messier, and it works.
  4. 4Nothing on paper? Then ask a question instead. “Which fifty medicines do you sell most?” A dispenser can answer that from memory in ten minutes, and those fifty are the ones the doctor writes every evening. Write them down with strengths.

What to say

“The doctor is starting to print his prescriptions instead of writing them by hand, so the chemist can read them. I need to know how the medicine names are spelled here, and the strengths. Only the names and the strengths. I am not writing down any prices, and I am not asking what you paid or what you sell for.”

Promise this, never that

Safe to say

  • The prices get deleted. They are not stored anywhere.
  • Nothing about any patient is being collected, today or later.
  • The list is used for spelling, so a printed slip matches the box on his shelf.
  • He will be able to read every prescription that comes to his counter.

Never say

  • That patients will be sent to his shop.
  • That he is the only shop, or the first, or a partner.
  • A date for the inventory system, or that he gets it free.
  • Any share of anything. There is nothing to share yet.
  • Anything about what the doctor prescribes or how much he earns.

Photographing a page

DoPage flat on the counter. Phone straight above it, not at an angle. Daylight or the shop's own light, flash off. One photo per page, in order. Check the strength column is readable before you move on.
Do notPhotograph at an angle to fit more in. Use flash on a glossy price list. Take one wide shot of four pages. Zoom with the camera instead of moving closer. Photograph a page with a customer's name on it.

A blurred strength is the one failure that matters. 500 read as 50 is a wrong prescription, and nobody downstream can tell it was the photograph.

Coming back

Paste or send the pages into Setup → Market. Prices, pack sizes, company names and quantity columns are stripped there and thrown away; brand, strength and form survive. Then each row gets its formula from the WHO list, and the Sindhi follows from the formula. Nothing reaches a printed slip until a person has ticked it.
