# The pilot

Six weeks. One clinic. This is the only thing that matters until it is done.

Written in plain English on purpose. Print it and keep it with you.

---

## Why this comes before everything

The app works. It has never been used with a real patient.

Everything we have planned — the hospital system, the pharmacy, the encryption,
the cloud — is guessing until one doctor uses this for six weeks. After six
weeks you will know things that no plan can tell you, and some of what we
have written will turn out to be wrong. That is fine. That is what a pilot is
for.

**So during these six weeks, build nothing new.** Fix what breaks. Nothing else.

---

## Before day one

Five things, and none of them take long.

**1. Put the app on the clinic's computer.** Not a browser tab. Install it so
it opens like a program, and check it still opens when the internet is off.
Turn the wifi off and open it. If it opens, you are ready.

**2. Print one slip on the clinic's own printer.** The real one, not yours.
Look at it. Is the Sindhi right? Is anything cut off at the edge? Do this
before a patient is waiting.

**3. Put the doctor's real medicines in.** Thirty to fifty is enough — the ones
he writes every day. Sample medicines are not his vocabulary and he will
notice within an hour.

**4. Get the Sindhi names checked.** The app will not print a Sindhi medicine
name until a person has ticked it. If nothing is ticked, the slip prints in
English only — and English-only is not the product. Pirah needs to go through
the list once before day one.

**5. Show the doctor and the compounder how to use it. Once.** Twenty minutes.
Then stop helping. If they need you standing there, the software is not ready,
and it is better to learn that in week one than in month six.

---

## Week one: measure two things

Only two. Both are quick and both have been outstanding for months.

### A. How long from tapping PRINT to paper in his hand

Use your phone's stopwatch. Do it ten times, on the clinic's real printer,
including the first print of the day when the printer is cold.

Write down all ten times.

**Why it matters:** he writes a prescription by hand in about twenty seconds.
If our way takes longer than that, he goes back to the pen — not on day one,
but on the evening the queue is forty deep. If any print takes more than about
eight seconds, tell me and I will fix it before week two.

### B. Do the pictures actually work

This is the important one. **This is the only thing about Nuskho that nobody
else has**, and it has never been tested on one human being.

Use the separate sheet — *Does the picture work?* — and fill in twenty patients.

The method matters, so follow it exactly:

- Hand the patient the slip. **Say nothing.**
- Point at one medicine row. Ask: *"Hee dawa kiyan khaana aahe?"* — how will you
  take this medicine?
- Listen. Do not help. Do not nod. Do not correct until after you have written
  down what they said.
- Write down their actual words, not "correct" or "wrong".

If they get it wrong, **that is the most valuable information in this project.**
Write down exactly what they thought it meant. A picture that means "three times
a day" being read as "three tablets at once" is the one way this software can
hurt somebody, and finding it now costs nothing.

## Weeks two to six: one question

Every week, ask yourself one thing and write the answer down:

> **Is he still switching the computer on without me reminding him?**

That is the whole test. Not whether he likes it. Not what he says when you ask —
he will be polite, he is a doctor in Larkana and you are a young man who built
something. Watch what he does when you are not there.

Also keep a simple count each week:

| | Week 2 | Week 3 | Week 4 | Week 5 | Week 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Patients seen | | | | | |
| Slips printed | | | | | |
| Times he used the pen instead | | | | | |

The last row is the one that tells the truth. **Ask the compounder, not the
doctor** — the compounder sees the pen come out and has no reason to be polite
about it.

---

## What "passing" means

Decide this now, before you are emotionally invested in the answer.

**The pilot passes if, in weeks four to six, at least four out of every five
patients get a printed slip.** Not week one — anybody uses a new thing in week
one. Weeks four to six, when it is boring and the queue is long.

And one of these two must also be true:

- he pays something, at whatever rate you agree, **or**
- he says clearly why he will not, and you write down his exact words

**If it fails, that is a success too.** A failed pilot in six weeks costs you six
weeks. Building a hospital system on a guess costs you three years.

---

## Rules while the pilot runs

**Change nothing except what is broken.** No new features, no redesign, no
encryption, no cloud, no pharmacy. If the doctor asks for something, write it
on a list and say "after six weeks". The list itself is valuable — it tells you
what a real doctor actually wants, which is worth more than everything we have
guessed so far.

**Do not defend the software.** When he complains, write it down and say thank
you. Arguing teaches you nothing.

**Visit once a week, at 6pm, and sit quietly.** Not a demo. Not a training
session. Sit at the side and watch the evening happen. You will see three things
in one hour that six weeks of messages would never tell you.

**Write one line every day.** Date, what happened, what broke. Two minutes. In
week seven this will be the most useful document you own.

---

## Things that will probably go wrong, so you are not surprised

- **The printer asks a question every time.** Chrome must be started with
  `--kiosk-printing` or a dialog appears for every patient. At 140 patients an
  evening that alone kills the pilot. Check this on day one.
- **A blank second page.** This was fixed, but check it on the clinic's real
  printer and real paper size.
- **The power goes.** It will. The app is built for this and loses nothing —
  but watch what the compounder does in that minute, because that is the part
  we have never seen.
- **The doctor's own name or degrees are wrong on the slip.** Fix it in Setup
  in one minute. But check before day one, because a doctor whose name is
  printed wrong will not use it a second time.
- **Somebody asks for the app.** Another doctor will hear about it. Say the
  pilot finishes in six weeks and take his number. Do not install a second
  clinic in the middle of this — two clinics is not twice the learning, it is
  half the attention.

---

## What happens in week seven

We sit down with what you wrote and decide the next twelve months from evidence
instead of guessing. All the planning documents get re-read against what
actually happened, and some of them get thrown away.

That is the point.
