This is the only thing about Nuskho that nobody else has, and it has never been tested on one human being. Twenty patients is enough to know.

How to ask

  1. Hand the patient the printed slip. Say nothing about it.
  2. Point at one medicine row — the picture, not the words.
  3. Ask the question below, and then be quiet.
  4. Write down their actual words before you say anything back.
  5. Only after you have written it down, tell them the right answer.
“How will you take this medicine?”
Hee dawa kiyan khaana aahe?
هيءَ دوا ڪيئن کائڻي آهي؟
A wrong answer is the most valuable thing you will collect. A picture meaning “three times a day” read as “three tablets at once” is the one way this software can hurt somebody. Finding it now costs nothing. Finding it in year two costs a patient.

Rules

  • Do not help. No hints, no nodding, no pointing at the number.
  • Do not ask “do you understand?” Everybody says yes. Make them tell you what it means.
  • Do not skip the ones who cannot read. They are the reason this exists.
  • If a family member answers instead, note it — and ask the patient anyway.
  • Ask about two or three medicines per patient, not all of them. People get tired and start guessing.

Marking

✔ right — they said the correct timing and the correct amount.
✘ wrong — they said something else. Write what they said.
? none — they could not say, or only guessed from the number.

Sheets 2–6 hold four patients each. Take all five to the clinic.
